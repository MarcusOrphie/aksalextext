YouTube Shorts Scripts with AI

Alex Aksenov · September 15, 2026 · 5 min read

How Shorts differ from Reels for a script

On YouTube viewers more often arrive through search and subscriptions, not just through the recommendations feed. That means the title and the first frame work as the video's thumbnail - they're visible before the view, and the click depends on them too.

The second difference is that YouTube viewers are used to longer videos on the platform overall, so Shorts have a slightly higher tolerance for a slow build at the start than Reels do. That's no reason to stretch out the intro, but a couple of seconds of context works better here than on other platforms.

Script structure: three mandatory blocks

  • The hook (0-2 seconds) - states the problem or the result people stay to see
  • The body - one point per video, no trying to cram in three topics at once
  • The ending - a conclusion, a question to the viewer or a lead-in to the next video in the series

The beginners' mistake is writing the script linearly, from start to finish. It's faster to work in reverse: first the ending and the main conclusion, then the hook built for that conclusion, and only then the body, which connects the two.

How AI helps with the script

Give the AI the topic, the video's key point and 2-3 examples of good hooks from your niche - it'll offer several structure options to choose from. Then you pick one and rewrite the text in your own way of speaking.

AI quickly produces structure options, but it doesn't know what actually landed with your audience last week. That part of the work still falls to the author.

AI is separately useful for drafting subtitles and on-screen text - it condenses long script text into short phrases, which by hand takes more time than the shoot itself.

Common mistakes in Shorts scripts

Too many points in one video

One idea per video works better than three. If the topic is big, split it into a series of several Shorts, each with a logical ending.

An ending with no payoff

A video that ends mid-sentence with no conclusion loses some retention in the final seconds. Even a short closing line keeps the viewer to the end better than an abrupt cutoff.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a Shorts script fundamentally different from a Reels one?

The difference isn't the length but the audience behavior: on YouTube viewers more often seek out a video deliberately, so the title and first frame work as part of the click, not just retention.

How long should the hook be?

Usually the first 1-2 seconds decide whether the viewer stays or scrolls on. Stretching the intro longer than that is risky for retention.

Can AI write a finished script without my input?

It'll put together a working structure draft, but without your point, examples and way of speaking the text comes out generic. The final version is still refined by the author.

Do Shorts need subtitles if the video has sound?

Yes, some viewers watch with the sound off, especially in the first seconds. Subtitles or on-screen text noticeably improve watch-through.

How do I tell that a script turned out to be a good one?

By the retention stats after publishing: if viewers drop off en masse in the first seconds, the problem is the hook; if it's closer to the middle, it's the structure of the body.
