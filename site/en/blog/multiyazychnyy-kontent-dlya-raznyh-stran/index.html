Multilingual Content: Posts for Different Countries

Alex Aksenov · September 19, 2026 · 5 min read

Translation and adaptation are different tasks

Translation preserves the meaning of the words. Adaptation preserves the effect the post was written for - to hook, to amuse, to build trust. If in the original you address the reader with a joke about local life, a literal translation turns it into a baffling string of words for someone in another country.

AI does a great job translating facts - numbers, instructions, product descriptions. It handles humor, idioms, and cultural references worse, and there's no getting around hands-on editing here.

What you can trust AI with, no edits needed

  • Technical descriptions, specs, instructions
  • Answers to common questions with no emotional coloring
  • A rough translation of a long text for later editing
  • Finding synonyms and simplifying complex phrasing

This is the case where speed matters more than artistry: AI gives you a working base in seconds, and you spend time only on the final proofread rather than translating from scratch.

What you absolutely must check by hand

Post elementRisk with machine translationWhat to do
Jokes and punsLose their meaning or sound oddReplace with a local equivalent or cut them
Dates and measurementsThe format confuses the readerConvert to the system familiar in that country
Forms of address and toneToo formal or too familiarCheck against the norms of communication in that culture
References to local realitiesMeaningless outside the countryReplace with a universal example
A good translation is invisible to the reader. They don't think 'this was translated', they just understand the post as easily as the original.

How to set up a process so you're not translating every post from scratch

Build a glossary of recurring terms

The product name, key phrases, frequently used expressions - put them in a separate list with a ready translation. Then AI will use the same phrasings each time instead of inventing new ones.

Check the tone with a real person

If you know a native speaker or someone from the target country, show them your finished translations from time to time. Even five minutes of feedback once a month saves you from awkward phrasings you'd never catch yourself.

Don't translate everything

Not every post needs another language. Personal stories and local humor are often better left as they are; translate universal formats: guides, lists, reviews.

The mistakes that most often ruin the impression

Even with a good translation, you can spoil the impression with organizational details.

  • A different tone in the original and the translation - as if two different people wrote them
  • Forgotten untranslated bits of interface or captions on screenshots
  • Ignoring the time zone and the relevance of news for another country
  • Translating without adapting currency and prices in examples

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you fully trust AI with translating posts?

For factual information, yes; for emotional and humorous content, no. Such texts need to be reread and adapted by hand.

At what share of foreign audience does it make sense to translate content?

If a single country steadily brings in 10-15% of your audience, it's already worth trying to adapt at least some of your posts for them.

How do you check that a translation sounds natural?

Show it to a native speaker or someone who has lived in that country for a long time. A machine check can't replace a live reaction to the text.

Do you need to translate absolutely every post?

No. Personal stories and local references are often better left in the original language only, while you translate universal formats like guides and reviews.

How do you handle humor when translating into another language?

A literal translation of a joke almost never works. It's easier to replace it with an equivalent that makes sense in the reader's culture, or to cut it entirely if there's no equivalent.
