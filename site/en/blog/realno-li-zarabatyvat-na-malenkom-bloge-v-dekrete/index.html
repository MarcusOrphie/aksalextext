Can You Really Earn From a Small Blog on Maternity Leave

Alex Aksenov · September 9, 2026 · 5 min read

Why it seems a small blog doesn't earn

On social media, the bloggers in the spotlight are the ones with huge numbers - they're the ones who most often make it into recommendations and conversations. That creates the impression that income only begins after a certain follower count. In reality, many small blogs with an active, trusting audience earn more than large channels with indifferent followers.

What matters to advertisers and buyers isn't just the number under your profile picture, but whether people read you closely and act on what you recommend.

What income actually depends on, and it isn't audience size

  • How narrow and clear your topic is - it's easier to find both an advertiser and a product for it
  • How actively your audience comments, saves, and sends you direct messages
  • Whether you have personal experience or expertise that can be packaged into a product or a consultation
  • Consistency - an irregular blog is harder to monetize, even if the occasional reach is good

What keeps you from starting to earn sooner

Most often what holds you back isn't audience size but the thought 'I'm not ready yet, I have too few followers.' While you wait for the perfect number, you lose the time you could have already spent testing your first small product.

I kept putting off my first guide until I hit a thousand followers. I made it at three hundred - five people bought it. It turned out it wasn't about the number, but about finally being able to articulate how I could be useful.

The second common obstacle is the fear of selling

Many people feel that offering something paid to their followers is awkward. But if the product genuinely solves the audience's problem, it isn't pushiness, it's a service - the same as recommending a good doctor or a useful book.

Where to start this week

  • Write down what followers most often ask you in direct messages - it's a hint for a future product
  • Check whether there are small brands in your niche you could offer a barter or a paid integration
  • Put together one short guide or roundup on a topic where you have personal experience
  • Openly tell your followers you're preparing a product and ask what they'd find useful inside

Frequently Asked Questions

At what follower count can I start earning?

There's no exact number - it depends on the niche and audience engagement. Some bloggers get their first income at a few hundred followers, if the audience is active and trusts the author.

What should I choose first: ads or my own product?

Your own product is often easier to launch, because you don't have to wait for an advertiser to come along. You decide yourself when to make it and what to include.

Won't it scare followers off if I start selling something?

Some of the audience may leave, but usually the ones who genuinely find the product useful stay. Being open about why you're doing it reduces distrust.

Do I need a perfectly designed product for a first sale?

No. The first version can be a short text document with concrete value inside. You can improve the design after you see the format is in demand.

How do I figure out what product my audience specifically needs?

Look at the questions you're asked most often in comments and direct messages. It's a direct hint at the solution people are willing to pay for.
