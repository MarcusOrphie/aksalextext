How to Return to Your Career Through a Blog After Maternity Leave

Alex Aksenov · September 8, 2026 · 5 min read

Why a blog works better than a resume

A resume describes the past: where you worked, what you did. A blog shows the present - how you think about your profession now, whether you keep up with changes in the field, whether you can explain the complex in simple words. To an employer or client, that often says more than a list of job titles.

Another plus: a blog does not ask about the gap. It shows current knowledge directly, and the question "what were you doing all those years" answers itself once the answer is already visible in your posts.

Where to start when it feels like you have nothing to say

The first thought usually goes like this: everything changed during my leave, I have fallen behind, there is nothing to write about. In reality, the experience you built up before the break has not gone anywhere - it just needs structuring and framing through the field's current challenges.

You do not have to know everything new to begin. It is enough to honestly show what you are learning right now.

What to write about at the start

Post formatWhat it shows
Breaking down a case from past experiencePractical skills and thinking
An opinion on industry newsThat you follow the changes
The process of learning a new toolReadiness to learn and adapt
Answering a common question in your fieldExpertise in a specific topic

You do not have to go through all the formats in order - pick the one where you have something to say with confidence right now.

How to show competence, not just interest

What sets an expert's post apart from generic phrases

  • A concrete example instead of a general claim
  • Numbers or details from personal experience, if you have them
  • Acknowledging the topic's complexity instead of reducing it to a slogan
  • Answering a question people actually ask in this profession

Competence shows not in a confident tone but in the details only someone who has really worked with the topic would know.

How a blog reaches the right people

Publishing on the blog is only half the job. It is worth sharing posts in professional communities, chats with former colleagues, topical groups - wherever your target audience sits, not just your personal followers.

Over time the blog starts working on its own: people link to it, find it through a specific search query, and reach out to the author directly, skipping the stage of applying to a job posting.

How long it takes to see the first result

Do not expect a quick effect - a blog works like a cumulative showcase, not a one-off announcement. The first responses usually come after a few weeks of regular posting, and serious offers after a few months.

The key is not to quit at the first quiet week: results in this format come with a delay, but they build up steadily, unlike a one-time blast of resumes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I start a blog to return to my career after maternity leave?

With a breakdown of one case from your past experience or an answer to a common question in your field. You do not need to wait until you have gathered lots of new knowledge - start with what you already know how to do.

How do I show competence if I have not worked in a while?

Through concrete details and examples from practice, not general phrases. Competence shows in the small things only someone who has really worked with the topic would know.

Do I need to explain the reason for my career break in the blog?

No, that is not necessary. A blog shows current knowledge directly, and the question of the gap often resolves itself once it is clear you are on top of the topic now.

Where do I find readers if my blog has only just started?

In professional communities, chats with former colleagues and topical groups - the concentration of the right audience there is higher than among your personal followers.

How soon do the first results appear?

The first responses usually come after a few weeks of regular posting, and noticeable offers after a few months. It is a cumulative process, not a quick campaign.
