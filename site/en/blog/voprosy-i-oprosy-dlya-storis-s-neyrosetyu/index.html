Questions and Polls for Stories With AI

Alex Aksenov · September 16, 2026 · 4 min read

Why ask your audience anything in Stories at all

A question in Stories does two things at once: it lifts your reach through interactions and gives you material for future content. Readers' answers often contain ready-made post topics - the wording of their pain points and questions that would never have occurred to you alone.

The trouble is that coming up with a new question every day is tiring. That's where the habit of slapping on the same dull sticker with no audience reaction comes from.

Which question formats actually work

Not all questions drive engagement equally. Different formats solve different tasks, and it's worth alternating them rather than getting stuck on one.

  • An open question - gives you detailed answers and post topics, but few people reply
  • A choice between two options - almost everyone answers, because there's no thinking involved
  • A rating slider - good for light topics and a quick read on the audience's mood
  • A poll with answer options - handy when you need to make a decision together with your audience
  • A provocative question with a gentle debate - collects the most comments

Prompts that produce workable phrasings

The AI's job is to offer options from which you pick the right tone. A generic request like 'come up with a question for Stories' usually gives you something bland, so it's worth specifying the context.

  • "Come up with 10 open questions about [topic] for an audience of [description], no cliches like 'how are you'"
  • "Come up with 5 two-option questions on the topic of [topic], both options should be realistic"
  • "Come up with a provocative question on [topic] that gently splits the audience into two camps"
  • "Reword this question to be shorter and livelier, drop the stiff, formal words"
A question in Stories works not because it's clever, but because it's easy to answer in a second without leaving the feed.

How to use your audience's answers afterward

The answers you collect aren't a one-time bit of fun, they're raw material for your next content. Different question formats yield material of different value.

Question formatWhat it gives you once answers are in
Open questionReady-made post topics and quotes for screenshots
Choice of twoAn understanding of which topic angle interests the audience more
Poll with optionsAn argument for the next post: 'you chose this topic yourselves'
ProvocationComments you can unpack in a separate post

Common mistakes with questions in Stories

Even a good question loses its effect if you make one of the typical mistakes.

  • Asking a question with no context - the audience doesn't understand what it's even about
  • Placing the question sticker on a slide crowded with text next to it
  • Asking the same thing every day - the audience stops noticing
  • Not showing the audience's answers - people reply more willingly when they see they're being read
  • Using provocation too often - it only works if it isn't your only format

How not to turn Stories into a stream of identical questions

Questions drive engagement, but if you put them in every other Story, the audience gets tired of answering. Better to keep a balance: one or two interactive stickers a day, the rest regular content with no call to act.

Once a week it's worth reviewing which question formats got the most answers and dropping the ones that stopped working. The audience gets used to a format over time, and what landed a month ago may no longer catch on.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many questions can you post in Stories per day?

One or two interactive stickers a day is usually enough. More than that, and the audience starts swiping through Stories without looking at the content.

Which question format gets the most answers?

A choice between two options and a slider usually collect the most answers, because they don't require any written text. An open question gets fewer replies, but the answers are more substantial.

How do you tell that a question fell flat?

If there are noticeably fewer reactions than usual, or the answers are identical and short, the wording was too general. It's worth narrowing the topic or clarifying the context.

Can you use audience questions as topics for posts?

Yes, that's one of the main reasons to ask them. The wording from the answers often describes the audience's pain points more precisely than any guesses the author could make.

Do you need to reply to every comment under a poll?

Not necessarily every one, but it's worth showing a few genuine answers in your Stories - it boosts engagement in future posts.
