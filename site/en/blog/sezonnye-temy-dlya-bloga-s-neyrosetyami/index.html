Seasonal Blog Topics: How to Find Them With AI

Alex Aksenov · September 15, 2026 · 4 min read

Why seasonality matters for a blog

Seasonal content catches demand that already exists in people's heads - before the school year starts, parents look for ideas for their kids; before New Year, everyone tallies up the year and makes plans; in spring, the audience takes an interest in refreshing their wardrobe or renovating. You don't need to create interest from scratch, you just need to be in the right topic at the right time.

The catch is that seasonal posts need to be prepared in advance - 2-4 weeks before the peak of interest, while the algorithms and search engines have time to show the content to the right audience.

How AI helps you find seasonal hooks

Ask the AI to put together a list of news hooks and holidays for the next 2-3 months in your niche - it will gather both the obvious dates and the less visible ones (the start of the school year, vacation season, the end of the quarter). After that your job is to pick what genuinely applies to your blog's topic.

  • Describe your blog's niche and audience to the AI briefly and specifically
  • Ask for a list of hooks 2-3 months ahead, not the whole year at once
  • Mark which hooks your audience definitely has and which are a stretch
  • Plan the publication for 2-4 weeks before the peak of interest in the topic

Seasonality isn't only about holidays

Seasonality isn't only New Year and International Women's Day. Every niche has its own cycle: the school year, financial quarters, vacation season, the start and end of the work year.

How to build seasonal topics into your content plan

Don't turn your whole plan into a set of seasonal hooks - the audience should still feel that the blog lives by its own topic, not just by the holiday calendar. A workable ratio is one seasonal post per 4-5 regular ones.

Draw up the list of hooks a quarter ahead rather than as the dates arrive. That leaves time to prepare the material calmly instead of in the last week before the holiday.

Common mistakes with seasonal content

Publishing on the day itself

By the holiday itself, the audience's interest is already fading - they start searching and preparing in advance. The best window for publishing is 2-4 weeks before the peak.

A hook for the sake of a hook

Not every date on the calendar fits your blog's topic. If a hook is a stretch, the post looks formal and doesn't get engagement, even if the topic is technically timely.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far ahead of a holiday should I publish seasonal content?

Usually 2-4 weeks before the peak of interest in the topic - by then the audience is already looking for ideas and information but hasn't yet switched to the holiday itself.

Can AI pick suitable seasonal topics on its own?

It will gather a list of hooks, but the decision about which ones fit your blog's audience stays with the author - AI doesn't know your niche's context as well as you do.

How many seasonal posts should there be in a content plan?

A good benchmark is one seasonal post per 4-5 regular ones, so the blog doesn't turn into a holiday calendar and keeps its core topic.

Which seasonal hooks are often missed besides holidays?

The start of the school year, vacation season, financial quarters, the start and end of the business year - every niche has its own non-holiday but regular cycle of demand.

How do I quickly draw up a list of seasonal topics for the quarter?

Ask the AI to gather hooks in your niche for the next 2-3 months, then spend 15-20 minutes picking from the list what genuinely applies to your blog and spreading it across publishing dates.
